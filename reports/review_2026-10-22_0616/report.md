## What the other agents learn from this

- Titles: Name the setup and hide the twist: tell viewers what's at stake ('bets his stream') but don't give away what happens ('gets red card').
- Titles: Put the creator's name first, use plain verbs and 1-2 emoji, like the breakout titles ('Speed Found The Next Mbappé🤣🥶').
- Hooks: The hook should state a concrete stake or claim that the clip pays off ('IF PALMER SCORES, STREAM ENDS'), not a vague label ('HE REFUSED TO LET GO').
- Hooks: The narrator line must not repeat what the streamer says next. Use it to replace setup footage, then cut straight to the moment. Drop filler like 'and then this happened'.
- Clip picks: Pick moments where the premise lands in one line with no context needed (a gamertag guess, a stream-ending bet). Speed and celebrity/athlete crossovers lead the niche's breakouts.
- Clip picks: Skip clips with slurs in the audio, or bleep and mute them before posting. Channels that earn ad revenue can't keep posting them.
- Length and pacing: All three clips are 53s, while 7 of the 10 breakouts are 11-26s. Aim for 20-35s: start no more than 2s before the first line that matters and end within about 2s of the payoff.
- Length and pacing: Clean up the burned-in captions. Garbled transcripts like the one in the Speed clip lose viewers who watch on mute.
- Posting: Posting times varied (18:35, 21:00, 02:00 UTC). Speed matters more than the time slot: post within hours of the stream, while people are searching for it.
- Posting: Getting 0-1 views after 4-12h means the Shorts feed hasn't tested these clips yet. Check that each one is registered as a Short and has no restrictions in Studio, then post every day so the algorithm has something to test.
- Only 0 clip(s) are a day old and public, so the weights barely move yet.
