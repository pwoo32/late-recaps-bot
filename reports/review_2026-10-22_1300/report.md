## What the other agents learn from this

- Titles: Put the creator first, describe the setup, and hide the twist. 'Speed Bets His Stream...' works; '...Gets a Red Card' gives the moment away.
- Titles: Use plain verbs plus 1-2 emoji, like the breakout titles ('Speed Found The Next Mbappé🤣🥶'). Only claim what the audio shows.
- Hooks: Make the hook a concrete stake or a countdown the clip pays off ('IF PALMER SCORES, STREAM ENDS'), not a mood label ('HE REFUSED TO LET GO').
- Hooks: The narrator states the promise or setup and never the result. Cut filler like 'and then this happened' and lines that give the joke away ('lasted one gamertag').
- Clip picks: Pick moments where the premise lands in one line with no context (a rank guess off a gamertag, a stream-ending bet). Speed and celebrity/athlete crossovers lead this niche.
- Clip picks: Listen to the full audio before posting. Mute or bleep slurs, or skip the clip, because ad-revenue channels can't keep posting them.
- Length and pacing: All three clips are 53s, while 7 of the 10 breakouts are 11-26s. Aim for 20-35s.
- Length and pacing: Start no more than 2s before the first line that matters and end within about 2s of the payoff. Trailing banter (Jynxzi's CS:GO joke, Speed's 'GG's', Adin's yelling) is where viewers leave.
- Length and pacing: Fix the burned-in captions by hand when the speech is fast or slurred. A garbled caption is a reason to swipe on mute.
- Posting: Post within hours of the stream, while people are searching for it. Speed of posting matters more than the time slot.
- Posting: At 0-1 views after 11-18h, the Shorts feed likely hasn't tested these clips yet. Check in Studio that each one is a Short with no restrictions, then post daily so there's something to test. Judge each clip on 3s retention and share of views from the feed after 48h.
